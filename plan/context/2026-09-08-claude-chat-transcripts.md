Yes, but with three qualifications that matter for the argument you're building.

The law does mandate it. Article 14 of the EU AI Act requires that high-risk AI systems be designed so that natural persons can monitor, understand, intervene in, and halt the system while it is in use. Article 26(2) goes further and requires deployers to assign qualified personnel with the appropriate authority, competence, and support to carry out that oversight. So there is a genuine, legally compelled human-in-the-loop function, which is the regulatory-pressure mechanism I described as the defensible end.

The qualifications:

First, it is narrower than "a human checks everything." Article 14 does not require a human to review every AI decision before it takes effect; it requires that humans have the capability to monitor, understand, intervene in, and halt the system. Prior human review is only mandatory in specific cases, such as biometric identification, where no decision can be taken unless the identification has been separately verified by at least two competent natural persons. And it only applies to high-risk systems, not AI generally.

Second, the role this creates is not specifically an HCI job. The mandated function is an oversight or governance role that can be filled by compliance staff, domain experts, or risk officers. HCI and human factors are relevant backgrounds for designing the oversight mechanisms, but the law does not reserve the work for that credential.

Third, the floor is softer and later than it looks right now. The EU just agreed to delay the high-risk obligations: under the Digital Omnibus, standalone Annex III high-risk obligations move from August 2026 to December 2027, a sixteen-month deferral, and AI embedded in regulated products under Annex I moves to August 2028. The package also narrowed scope, clarifying that AI systems that only assist users or optimise performance will not automatically be high-risk if their failure does not create health or safety risks. As of now this is a provisional political agreement, not yet formally adopted.

So the law supports the regulatory-pressure case in general. It does not specifically shield an HCI degree, and the timeline and scope are being relaxed, which is the opposite direction from a hardening protective moat.

Spanish first is the easier path.

- Practical factor: Catalan resources for English speakers are thin, and most beginner Catalan material assumes Spanish or is taught through Spanish.

No separate apps or terminal windows. Agents (subagents) live inside a single Claude Code session: the main agent spawns isolated Claude instances that run in parallel, each with its own context window, tool permissions, and model, and the lead agent only sees each one's final summary.

You organize them as files:

- Project subagents go in `.claude/agents/` in your repo; user-level subagents go in `~/.claude/agents/` and are available in all your projects. Both are scanned recursively, so you can group them into subfolders like `agents/review/` or `agents/research/`
- Each one is a markdown file with YAML frontmatter defining name, description, tools, and model
- The main Claude delegates to them automatically based on the description field, or you can type @ and pick the subagent directly to guarantee that specific one runs

So one terminal, one session. Claude orchestrates the agents for you behind the scenes.

Full docs: https://code.claude.com/docs/en/sub-agents

**Floridi: The Core Architecture**

Everything in the book hangs off three ideas.

**1. AI is a divorce of agency from intelligence**

- Floridi's central move: AI is not artificial intelligence in any meaningful sense. It is artificial *agency*. These systems act successfully in the world without understanding anything.
- Historically, effective action required intelligence. AI decouples them. A translation model translates without knowing any language.
- Consequence: the sci-fi framing (machines becoming minds) is a distraction. The real story is that we are filling the world with agents that have zero intelligence but enormous capacity to act.
- Companion concept, **enveloping**: we don't make AI smart enough for our world. We reshape the world to fit AI's limits, the way a factory builds an "envelope" around a robot arm. Recommendation engines, standardized data, smart homes: all enveloping. The ethical question becomes who controls the envelope and who gets shaped by it.

**2. The information ethics foundation**

- Floridi's larger project is the philosophy of information. Humans are "inforgs," informational organisms living in the "infosphere" alongside artificial agents.
- He calls the digital revolution the **fourth revolution**: Copernicus displaced us from the center of the universe, Darwin from the center of biology, Freud from the center of our own minds, and Turing from the center of the infosphere. We are no longer the only agents that process information and act on it.
- Ethics, on this view, is about the flourishing of the whole infosphere, not just human interests. This is why his framework extends beyond harm-to-humans thinking.

**3. The unified framework of principles**

- Floridi surveyed the major AI ethics guideline sets and found they converge on the four classic bioethics principles: **beneficence, non-maleficence, autonomy, justice**.
- His contribution: AI requires a fifth, **explicability**, which combines intelligibility (how does it work) and accountability (who is responsible). The first four principles can't operate if you can't answer those two questions.
- Two supporting distinctions you should know:
  - **Hard vs. soft ethics.** Hard ethics shapes law. Soft ethics is what you ought to do over and above compliance. His argument: in mature regulatory environments, the real ethical work is soft.
  - **The five ways ethics gets gamed**: ethics shopping (picking convenient principles), bluewashing (PR without substance), lobbying (using ethics to delay regulation), dumping (exporting unethical practice to weaker jurisdictions), shirking (doing less wherever accountability is low).

**One more frame he uses throughout**

- The risk of AI is not only misuse and overuse. It is also **underuse**: the opportunity cost of failing to deploy AI where it could reduce harm. He treats wasted potential as an ethical failure, which is unusual among AI ethicists and useful for your consulting framing.

## 19. LLC annual vs quarterly tax filing fees
URL: https://claude.ai/chat/0b555649-7a64-44ce-885a-f117d1a1f644
Last updated: 2026-03-29

### Human
As an llc, what fees do i pay for filing taxes annually instead of quarterly?

### Assistant
There's no direct "fee" for filing annually instead of quarterly, but there are **penalties** from the IRS if you underpay throughout the year.

**Underpayment Penalty**
If you owe $1,000+ at tax time and didn't pay enough quarterly, the IRS charges an underpayment penalty. Currently ~7-8% annualized (tied to the federal funds rate) on the amount you should have paid.

**Safe harbor to avoid the penalty:** pay whichever is less:
- 100% of last year's tax liability, or
- 90% of this year's tax liability

**Colorado also has estimated tax requirements:** same logic applies at the state level, with its own underpayment penalty if you skip quarterly payments.

**The practical reality for an LLC:**
- Single-member LLC (disregarded entity): you're paying as a sole proprietor, so quarterly estimates via Form 1040-ES apply to you personally
- If you're S-Corp elected: paying yourself a salary means withholding handles part of it, reducing your quarterly burden

There's no flat filing fee difference, it's purely penalty-based if you underpay. Worth running the numbers with your CPA on whether the penalty cost vs. cash flow benefit of holding funds quarterly makes sense for Superstoke's situation.

### Human
So i have to pay quarterly?

### Assistant
Not *required*, but if you expect to owe $1,000+ at year end and don't pay quarterly, you'll owe the underpayment penalty on top of your tax bill.

Practically speaking: most self-employed/LLC owners pay quarterly just to avoid the penalty and avoid a big lump sum in April. But if your tax liability is low enough (under $1k), or you have enough withholding from other income to cover it, you can skip quarterlies with no consequence.

### Human
What is the quarterly penalty

### Assistant
Currently **7% annualized** on the underpaid amount (IRS adjusts this quarterly based on the federal funds rate, it's been in the 7-8% range recently).

It's calculated per quarter, so it compounds if you miss multiple quarters. But it's not a flat fee, it's proportional to how much you underpaid and for how long.
